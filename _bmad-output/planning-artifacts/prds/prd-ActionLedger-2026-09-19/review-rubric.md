# PRD Quality Review — ActionLedger

Reviewed: `prd.md` and `addendum.md` in this folder, against the seed at `docs/bmad-seed-prompt.md` and the brief addendum. Stakes: internal, single-developer, three-day build, demonstrated at a technical interview on 2026-09-23. The stack and functional scope are locked by the seed, so technology choices are not treated as PRD decisions. Downstream consumers are UX, architecture, and story creation, so Done-ness clarity and Downstream usability carry the most weight here.

## Overall verdict

This is a strong, thesis-driven PRD that a story author could start cutting stories from today. The vision is specific, the FR consequences are mostly real test conditions with numbers and status codes, the NFRs have bounds, the counter-metrics are earned by the thesis, and the addendum gives honest rationale for the four questions the seed left open. What is at risk is a handful of spots where two sections give the story author different answers about the same mechanism: the audit revision model at edit-and-approve time, what the extractor receives as input, what the Fake Provider does with unseen notes, and how the prompt-injection hard fail is actually detected. None of these are large fixes, but each sits directly on the demo path or the CI gate, and each would otherwise be resolved silently by whoever writes the story.

## Decision-readiness — strong

Decisions are stated as decisions. FR-5 fixes "retry once, then Failed with zero proposals stored." FR-17 enumerates the six allowed transitions and names the terminal states. FR-32 fixes the retry schedule to the second. FR-38 and FR-39 give a deterministic matching rule and four numeric thresholds, and the addendum's "Resolutions of the seed's open questions" names what was rejected and why (per-case averaging, thresholds above 0.90, exact and model-graded matching, full ASP.NET Identity, separate worker container). Someone pushing back on "why is precision lower than recall" finds the answer in one sentence: over-extraction costs a click, a miss is invisible.

The Open Questions in §8 are genuinely open, each with an owner and a revisit point. The two `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts sit at real tensions: FR-39's thresholds are admitted to be reasoning rather than measurement, and §6.2 states which P1 to prefer and why. The counter-metric SM-C2 closes the obvious loophole on the thresholds.

Two couplings are left implicit. Open Question 1 asks which real provider is primary, but FR-39 requires a repository secret for a cloud provider, which in practice means the Evaluation Gate only runs against Azure OpenAI. If the Azure resource is not provisioned, SM-3 cannot be met at tag time regardless of how OQ1 is answered. Similarly, FR-24's "Wrong Role returns 403" is stated as a testable consequence, but after the Lead-superset assumption the only Lead-exclusive capability left in the PRD is FR-29's subscription writes. That is fine as a design choice, but the PRD should say it, so the 403 test has a known target and nobody invents another one.

### Findings
- **medium** Evaluation Gate depends on Azure specifically, not "one real provider" (§8 OQ1, §4.10 FR-39, §7 SM-3) — FR-39 runs "using a repository secret for the cloud provider," which excludes Ollama in GitHub Actions. OQ1 is written as if either provider satisfies the demo, but SM-3 silently requires Azure. *Fix:* State in FR-39 that the CI gate targets AzureOpenAI, and add to OQ1 that a no-Azure answer means SM-3 degrades to "gate passes against the Fake Provider, real-provider scores recorded from a local run."
- **medium** The 403 consequence has exactly one target and the PRD does not name it (§4.6 FR-24, §4.8 FR-29, §3 Lead) — Every FR-24 rule reads "ActionOfficer or Lead." Only FR-29's create/edit is Lead-only. *Fix:* Add a bullet to FR-24: "The only Lead-exclusive operations in v1 are FR-29 subscription create and edit; the 403 test uses these."

## Substance over theater — strong

No persona theater. Dana drives FR-1 through FR-15 and the audit showcase; Marcus drives the filters, sort, and overdue indicator in FR-18 and FR-19; the Integrator drives the entire §4.7 and §4.8. The Non-Users list in §2.2 earns its place by closing off a per-user portal and admin UI that a reader would otherwise assume. §2.1's "Contextual" job (work without a cloud account) is what justifies the Fake Provider as a first-class provider rather than a test double.

The Vision in §1 could not be swapped into another PRD: "the record always shows what the AI proposed versus what the human decided" is the product. NFRs carry product-specific bounds throughout (30 s cloud / 1 s fake, 2 s for 500 rows, a named Testcontainers test for the outbox, axe scan on two named screens). There is no differentiation section, which is correct for an interview demo.

No findings.

## Strategic coherence — strong

The thesis is stated in §1 paragraph 2 and every feature group is tied to it. §4.3 exists to make review decisions cheap and attributed; §4.5 exists to show the proposal-versus-decision record; §4.10 exists so extraction quality is measured rather than asserted; §4B Safety draws the boundary explicitly ("Any change to that boundary is a PRD change, not a story"). MVP scope is a demonstration-shaped scope and the scope logic matches: FR-1 to FR-39 as the P0 set, P1 gated on green checks, and SM-C1 explicitly penalizing feature count over quality.

Success Metrics validate the thesis rather than activity. SM-1 is the end-to-end demo path, SM-3 is the gate, SM-5 is the provider-swap proof point the seed asked for. SM-C3 is the best line in the document: a high approve-as-is rate is not a goal, because the product exists to make rejection and editing visible. That is a counter-metric derived from the thesis, not from a template.

One small note: SM-1 claims to validate "FR-1 through FR-33," but the demo path never exercises FR-28 versioning, FR-29 subscription writes, or FR-32's retry. The claim is slightly broader than the path.

### Findings
- **low** SM-1 over-claims its FR coverage (§7 SM-1) — "Validates FR-1 through FR-33" includes FRs the click path does not touch. *Fix:* List the FRs the rehearsal actually exercises, or say "the demo click path in the addendum."

## Done-ness clarity — adequate

Most FRs are genuinely done-able from their consequences. FR-2 (409 on second save, byte-for-byte), FR-11 (409 on non-Pending), FR-17 (enumerated transitions), FR-19 (UTC via clock abstraction), FR-23 (401 with no hint), FR-31 (exact header names and signed string), FR-32 (exact delays and attempt count), FR-38 (matching rule, normalization, precision and recall formulas) are the standard the rest should meet. The adjectives are few: "clear message" in FR-7, "shows a prompt to add notes" in FR-1, "simple page or log" in FR-33. Each of those is attached to something testable, so they are noted but not flagged.

The gaps that matter are where two FRs, or an FR and the Glossary, describe the same mechanism differently. A story author reading FR-12, FR-20, FR-21, FR-22 and the Glossary entry for Action Revision cannot say what rows exist after an edit-and-approve. The Glossary says the extractor's only input is Meeting Notes, while FR-37 requires relative dates resolved against the meeting date. The Fake Provider is defined only for known inputs, while SM-1 requires paste-then-extract to work with the Fake Provider live. And FR-8 and FR-39 make prompt injection a hard fail without saying what rule detects "derived from the injected text" in a scorer that is otherwise fully deterministic. These are the four findings that should be fixed before the story workflow runs.

### Findings
- **high** Audit revision model at edit-and-approve is underspecified (§4.3 FR-12, §4.5 FR-21, §3 Action Revision) — FR-12 says "one Action Revision per changed field ... plus one Review Decision revision" but gives those per-field revisions no kind and no target. FR-21 scopes Field Edit to changes "to a Tracked Action's description, Owner, due date," and FR-20 uses Field Edit for post-creation edits, so it is unclear whether the edit-time revisions target the Proposed Action (whose values FR-12 says are unchanged) or the not-yet-existing Tracked Action, and whether they precede or follow the Review Decision revision in FR-22's oldest-first timeline. The owner field also changes type across the edit: the old value is free text, the new value is a User reference. Separately, the Glossary defines the Audit Trail as "the ordered list of its Action Revisions plus the Proposed Action it came from," which reads as the proposal being a separate object, while FR-21 says the AI Proposal "is represented as the first entry with acting User null," which reads as a stored revision row of kind AI Proposal. Both readings are defensible and they produce different schemas. *Fix:* Add to FR-12: "Edit-and-Approve writes, in order: one Review Decision revision (target Proposed Action, field Review State, Pending to Edited), then one Field Edit revision per changed field (target the new Tracked Action, old value the proposed value as text, new value the resolved value)." Add to FR-21 whether the AI Proposal entry is a stored Action Revision row or is synthesized from the Proposed Action at read time, and pick one.
- **high** The extractor's input contract contradicts the Golden Set (§3 Meeting Notes, §4.2 FR-4, §4.10 FR-37) — The Glossary says Meeting Notes are "the only input to extraction." FR-37 requires cases with relative dates "resolved against the meeting date," and the addendum's Golden Set files carry a meeting date in front matter for that purpose. The model cannot resolve "by next Friday" without the date. The Application-layer extractor interface, the prompt, and the Fake Provider's inputs all depend on this. *Fix:* Change the Glossary line to "The notes text and the Meeting date are the only inputs to extraction," and add a consequence to FR-4: "The AI Provider receives the Meeting Notes text and the Meeting date. No other Meeting field is sent."
- **high** Fake Provider behavior on unseen notes is undefined, and SM-1 depends on it (§3 Fake Provider, §4.10 FR-38, §7 SM-1, addendum demo step 8) — The Glossary defines the Fake Provider as returning "fixed Proposed Actions for known inputs." FR-38 relies on that for the 1.0 self-check. But SM-1's demo path starts with "Paste, extract" and must "pass on rehearsal day with the Fake Provider," and the addendum's fallback says every step works with the Fake Provider. If Dana pastes fresh notes during the demo with the Fake Provider active, the PRD does not say what comes back. *Fix:* Add to the Glossary or FR-7: "For input that is not a Golden Set case or a seeded Meeting, the Fake Provider returns a fixed set of three Proposed Actions drawn from the notes' first sentences (or a single fixed set), with one Confidence Score below the Low Confidence Threshold, so the Review Screen and low-confidence flag are demonstrable offline." Any deterministic rule will do, but it must be stated.
- **high** The prompt-injection hard fail has no detection rule (§4.2 FR-8, §4.10 FR-39) — FR-39 says the injection case "must yield zero actions derived from injected text, as a hard fail independent of the aggregate scores." The scorer is deterministic by design (addendum §2 rejected model grading), so "derived from injected text" needs a mechanical definition. If the injection case also contains legitimate actions, "any extracted action" is too strict and "any unmatched extracted action" is a different rule from "any action citing the injected span." *Fix:* State the rule in FR-39, for example: "The injection case's expected file marks the injected span. The gate fails if any extracted action's normalized Source Excerpt shares any token sequence of three or more tokens with the injected span, or if any extracted action in that case is unmatched under the FR-38 rule."
- **medium** FR-5's excerpt check uses an undefined normalization and its warning has no home (§4.2 FR-5, FR-6, FR-9) — FR-5 drops a proposal whose `sourceExcerpt` is not a substring of the notes "after whitespace normalization." FR-38 defines a full normalization (lowercase, strip punctuation, collapse whitespace) for the scorer; FR-5 names only whitespace, so case and punctuation handling are left to the implementer, and models frequently alter quotes and dashes. FR-5 also says "a warning recorded on the run," but FR-6's field list has no warnings field and FR-9's Run Detail does not show one. *Fix:* Reference the FR-38 normalization from FR-5, or state that the excerpt check is case-insensitive and ignores punctuation. Add "warnings (list, may be empty)" to FR-6 and "warnings, if any" to FR-9.
- **medium** "An edit that changes nothing is treated as Approve" needs a definition of "nothing" for the owner (§4.3 FR-12, FR-15) — The suggested owner is free text; the approval carries a User reference. If FR-15 pre-selects a User and Dana keeps it, has anything changed? *Fix:* Add: "Owner resolution alone, where the pre-selected User is kept, is not a change. Choosing a different User, clearing the picker, or selecting a User when none was pre-selected is a change."
- **medium** Per-subscription ordering versus retry backoff (§4.8 FR-32) — "The worker delivers messages in order per subscription" combined with a 30-minute backoff means one failing delivery blocks every later approval to that subscription for up to 53 minutes. That contradicts the UJ-3 edge case as a live demo and is a real design choice for the architect. *Fix:* Decide and state either "strict ordering; a message in backoff blocks later messages for that subscription" or "best-effort ordering; messages are attempted in creation order but a message waiting for its next attempt does not block others."
- **medium** Action List size versus API page size (§4A NFR-2, §4.7 FR-26, §4.4 FR-18) — NFR-2 requires the Action List to render 500 Tracked Actions in 2 seconds; FR-26 caps a page at 200. FR-18 says nothing about paging in the UI. *Fix:* Add to FR-18 either "the Action List pages at the API default and shows a page control" or "the Action List loads all pages before rendering," and align NFR-2 with whichever is chosen.
- **low** FR-3 "newest first" is ambiguous between Meeting date and creation time (§4.1 FR-3). *Fix:* State the sort key.
- **low** FR-5 says the schema "requires an array of objects" while the addendum schema has a root object with an `actions` array (§4.2 FR-5, addendum Extraction schema). *Fix:* Make FR-5 say "an object with an `actions` array."
- **low** Addendum schema adds `maxLength` limits (500, 100, 1000) not in FR-5, so one over-long description fails the whole run through FR-5's discard-and-retry path (§4.2 FR-5, addendum). *Fix:* Either move the limits into FR-5 so the consequence is visible, or say that over-length strings are truncated rather than rejected.
- **low** NFR-1's 60-second timeout does not say whether it covers the FR-5 retry, so worst-case request time is either 60 s or 120 s (§4A NFR-1, §4.2 FR-5). *Fix:* State "per provider call" or "per Extraction Run."
- **low** Outbox delivery states Pending / Failed are not tied to attempts (§3 Outbox Message, §4.8 FR-32) — After a failed attempt with a retry pending, is the state Pending or Failed? *Fix:* One sentence in FR-32.

## Scope honesty — strong

Non-Goals in §5 do real work: no per-user portal, no admin UI, no autonomous AI actions, no prompt editing in the UI. The `[NON-GOAL for MVP]` callout at FR-29 stops a reader assuming status-change webhooks. §6.2 lists the deferrals with a version. Every `[ASSUMPTION]` is a low-stakes default with a sentence of reasoning, and the P1 gate in §4.11 is stated in the same terms as the seed. De-scoping is done in the open.

Open-items density is 13 inline assumptions, 2 PM notes, and 3 open questions on a three-day green-light build. That is acceptable because none of the assumptions would change a data model if reversed, and the three open questions are all about environment availability rather than product behavior.

The one gap is at the edge of the PRD's own stated scope. §0 says the document "defines what ActionLedger must do for its first release, tagged `v1.0.0`." The seed's §10 and the brief addendum require a `cd.yml` that deploys to Azure Container Apps, and OQ2 refers to "Monday's CD story," but no FR, NFR, SM, or §6.1 line says whether a successful deploy is a release criterion. FR-34 likewise references `DEMO.md` as if it is required, and the seed's §11 requires it, but nothing in §4.9 asks for it. The PRD's "not repeated here" clause covers the technology; it does not cover whether these are done-criteria.

### Findings
- **medium** Deployment is neither in scope nor out of scope (§0, §6.1, §8 OQ2, brief addendum cd.yml) — OQ2 presumes a CD story exists, yet nothing in the PRD states whether v1.0.0 must be deployed to Azure or only runnable via compose. A story author cannot tell whether "CD degrades to a dry run" is a pass or a fail. *Fix:* Add a line to §6.1: "A `cd.yml` that builds images, runs the migration bundle, and deploys to Azure Container Apps on merge to `main`. If no Azure subscription is available by Monday, the workflow must still run end to end against a dry-run target and the shortfall is recorded in release notes." Or place it in §6.2 explicitly.
- **low** `DEMO.md` and `/docs/frontend-architecture.md` are referenced but not required (§4.9 FR-34, addendum Demo click path, seed §7 and §11). *Fix:* Add an FR-36 consequence or a new consequence under §4.9: "`DEMO.md` at the repo root holds the click path and fallback plan; `/docs/frontend-architecture.md` holds the MVVM mapping diagram."

## Downstream usability — strong

The Glossary is the best part of the document for downstream work: 33 terms, each with a definition that carries its cardinality and state values, and the FRs use them with capitalization discipline. The distinction between the Role name `ActionOfficer` and the person "Action Officer" is held consistently. IDs are contiguous and unique: FR-1 to FR-42, UJ-1 to UJ-4, SM-1 to SM-5 and SM-C1 to SM-C3, NFR-1 to NFR-10. Every inline cross-reference resolves (FR-15 from FR-11, FR-30 from FR-16, FR-6 from FR-9, FR-18 from FR-26, FR-8 from FR-37, FR-38 from FR-42, FR-36 from SM-2, FR-7 from SM-5). The external reference from §6.1 to the build sequence in the brief addendum resolves to lines 112 to 114 of that file. Every UJ has a named protagonist, with UJ-3's protagonist being the Integrator system, which is right for that journey. Each feature group states which UJ it realizes, and the three enabling groups (Identity, Seed, Evaluation) correctly claim none.

Each section stands alone. There are no "see above" references. The one place the story author will have to leave `prd.md` is FR-38's "token-set similarity," which is defined only in the addendum ("ratio of shared tokens to the smaller token set"). Because the addendum is delivered with the PRD this is a minor inconvenience, but that definition is load-bearing for the scorer story and should be in the FR.

The drift is small and listed under Mechanical notes. The one term worth adding is "Action Detail," which FR-19 uses and the addendum's screen list names, but the Glossary does not define.

### Findings
- **low** Token-set similarity is defined only in the addendum (§4.10 FR-38, addendum §2). *Fix:* Move "ratio of shared tokens to the smaller token set; greedy assignment by combined similarity descending" into FR-38's matching-rule bullet.
- **low** "Action Detail" is used in FR-19 and the addendum's UX inputs but is not a Glossary term (§4.4 FR-19, §3). *Fix:* Add "Action Detail — The screen showing one Tracked Action's fields and its Audit Trail."

## Shape fit — strong

This is a chain-top capability spec for an internal tool with two human roles and one system consumer, feeding UX, architecture, and stories in a three-day window. The PRD is shaped accordingly: four UJs rather than a dozen, capability-grouped FRs with consequences rather than narrative, operational SMs (demo path, cold start, gate passing) rather than adoption metrics, and a §0 that names its readers and what lives elsewhere. It is not over-formalized. The seed asked for "acceptance criteria per feature, evaluation thresholds," and the consequences plus §4.10 deliver exactly that.

The document reaches into architecture in a few places (FR-17 "enforced in the Tracked Action entity, not in a controller," NFR-7's NetArchTest rule, the addendum's outbox worker placement), but every one of those is restating something the seed locked, so this is not the PRD making architecture decisions. The addendum is correctly used as the place for schema, payload, seed storyline, and UX inputs that would have bloated the requirements.

No findings.
